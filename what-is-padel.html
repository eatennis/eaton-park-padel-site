The basics

What is padel?

A fast-paced racket sport that combines the best of tennis and squash. Easy to learn, hard to put down.

How to play padel

[Padel racket photo — drop in images/whatis/racket.jpg]

Padel racket

Solid hitting surface with small holes to cut air resistance. Shorter handle than a tennis racket. Built for control over power.

[Padel shoes photo — drop in images/whatis/shoes.jpg]

Court shoes

Similar to tennis shoes with extra ankle support and reinforced soles for the quick changes of direction.

[Padel balls photo — drop in images/whatis/balls.jpg]

Padel balls

Lower-pressure than a tennis ball, so they bounce less, and slightly smaller too. Rackets and balls are free to borrow while you start out — request them on our racket request form at least 24 hours before you play.

  • Padel is played on a 20m × 10m court, in doubles, with glass back walls and metal sides.
  • If the ball flies directly into the opponent's glass wall on the full, it's out — it must bounce on the ground first.
  • You can hit a volley, a half-volley, or let the ball bounce on the glass before returning it.
  • You can also use your own back wall to play a return shot — bounce, then off the glass, then over.
  • If you hit the ball into the metal fence on your own side, you lose the point.
  • Serves are underarm, diagonal, and must bounce in the service box.
  • Matches are best of 3 sets; sets are first to 6 games with a 7-point tie-break at 6–6.

Padel uses the same scoring as tennis — 0, 15, 30, 40, game. If both pairs reach 40-all (deuce), you need a two-point lead to take the game.

To win a set, you need 6 games and a two-game lead. At 5-5 you play on; at 6-6 you play a tie-break — first to 7 points with a 2-point lead.

Matches are best of 3 sets. Win two and you win the match.

The walls mean rallies last longer than tennis, so games turn on tactics and patience more than power.

The serve is underarm, struck at or below waist height. Unlike tennis, you bounce the ball on the ground first, then strike it.

The ball must land in the opponent's diagonal service box. If it bounces and then hits the glass wall, it's still in — but if it hits the metal fence, it's a fault.

You get two serves like in tennis. Touch the net but land in the box? Re-serve, same as tennis lets.

The glass walls are what make padel feel different from tennis. When the ball lands on your side, you can let it bounce off your own back wall and then return it. You can even hit your own back wall on purpose to send the ball back over.

The metal fence on the sides can also keep the ball in play — as long as the ball bounces on the ground first.

If a ball flies directly into the opposing back wall or fence on the full (without bouncing first), it's out.

Padel vs tennis — what's different?

Smaller court, lower walls, doubles by default, glass back walls in play. Rackets are solid rather than strung, and points last longer because of the walls. It's more about teamwork and positioning than raw power — which is why beginners and seasoned tennis players often end up evenly matched after a few rallies.
